## What you may do without asking

**Nominative use.** Refer to the project by name to say what it is, what your
work builds on, or how your project relates to it. All of the following are
fine:

- "Built on the DAIR Containment Loop by IROC Security"
- "A fork of IROC Security's DAIR Containment Loop"
- "Compatible with the DAIR Containment Loop audit format"
- Citing the project in a blog post, talk, paper, or vendor comparison

**Attribution.** Keep the `NOTICE` file and copyright headers intact, as the
Apache License already requires. That is attribution, not trademark use, and it
is mandatory.

**Unmodified redistribution.** Distribute unmodified copies under the project
name, with the `LICENSE` and `NOTICE` files included.

## What requires permission

**Naming a modified version after this project.** If you change the code, give
your version its own name. You may say it is *derived from* or *based on* the
DAIR Containment Loop; you may not call it the DAIR Containment Loop. This is
the core of the policy: users must be able to tell whether they are running our
code or yours.

**Using IROC Security's name or logo to promote your product or service.**
Including in product names, domain names, app-store listings, package names,
company names, social handles, conference booths, or marketing material.

**Anything that implies endorsement, affiliation, certification, or

implementation," "IROC partner," and "powered by IROC Security" all require

**Merchandise** bearing the marks.

## A note on the spirit of this

Contributions are genuinely welcome, and forking is an expected, healthy part of
open source — the Apache License is here precisely so you can do that without
asking anyone. Nothing in this policy is intended to discourage you from using,
studying, adapting, or shipping this code.

What this policy protects is narrower: the ability of a security practitioner to
know whose code they are about to run against their production tenant. A tool
that isolates hosts and disables accounts is one where provenance matters. Keep
your name on your changes and ours on ours, and there is nothing here to trip
over.
